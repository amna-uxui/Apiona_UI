How to read the section

Rings are years
Léa sits at the centre. Each ring outward is one year, from her first symptoms in 2008 to today.
Wedges are body systems
They never move, so every patient’s record reads the same way and the eye learns where to look.
Cells are moments
A tinted cell is a symptom, a visit or a finding. Pale cells are years with nothing on file.
Texture is paperwork
The small cells inside a moment are its documents. The GP file is dense; the dermatology letter that mattered is two cells.

Four levels of zoom

  1. Overview. The whole history at a glance, with flagged moments named.
  2. Body system. Every moment in one wedge, labelled with its year.
  3. Moment. Its documents, quotes and measurements as cells.
  4. Source. The exact sentence the brief relied on, highlighted.

Rules for the team

  • Keep the map fixed. Wedge order and ring scale never change between patients.
  • Zoom instead of navigating. Going deeper is one gesture with a breadcrumb back, never a new page.
  • Every highlight has a source. Anything the brief flags opens the sentence it came from.
  • Keep the three voices apart. Léa, clinicians and the brief each have their own stain.
  • Plain words on labels. The garden lives in shape and motion. A label says “dismissal”, never a metaphor.
